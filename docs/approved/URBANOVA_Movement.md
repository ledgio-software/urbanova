1\. Purpose of This Document

URBANOVA started as a streetwear brand. It is now being registered as a business and run as a movement: a community of bold young people, backed by partner businesses, that helps youth be themselves, grow their own ventures, and get support when they need it.

This document sets out the vision, mission and values; what the movement will do; how other businesses can partner with it; how it will earn and raise money; and the policies that keep it trustworthy. It draws on how established youth-enterprise organizations operate (Section 10) and is a working draft for the founders to edit, not a legal document.

*It sits alongside the six website documents already produced. Section 12 notes which parts of the website would need to change to support the movement.*

2\. Vision, Mission & Values

2.1 Vision

> *A generation of young people who are proud to be exactly who they are, and who have the community, the platform and the support to build something of their own.*

2.2 Mission

> *To give young people the confidence, visibility and practical support to turn their ideas into real ventures, through a bold streetwear brand, a partner network of businesses, and a fund that helps those who need it most.*

2.3 What We Believe

- **Be yourself, loudly.** Young people should never have to shrink to fit. Boldness is the starting point, not the risk.

- **Courage is a team sport.** No one builds alone. The movement connects people with mentors, customers and each other.

- **Visibility is power.** A good idea nobody sees goes nowhere. We help young founders get seen.

- **Help is part of the job.** A share of what we earn goes back to young people who need a hand.

- **Straight dealing.** We are open about where money comes from and where it goes.

2.4 Core Values

| **Value**    | **What it means in practice**                                                                  |
|--------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Boldness     | We back young people who try, including when it is not polished yet                            |
| Authenticity | We encourage people to show up as themselves, not copy someone else's look                     |
| Community    | Members, partners and supporters look out for each other                                       |
| Integrity    | Honest reporting, fair partnerships, no misleading promises                                    |
| Opportunity  | Support is open to young people from any background, with priority for those with least access |

3\. Two Arms: The Brand and the Movement

The most important structural decision is to keep the money-making brand and the giving-back movement as two clearly separate arms, even if the same founders run both. This keeps customer sales, donations and grants from getting mixed up, and makes it easier to answer questions from donors, partners and tax authorities.

|                        | **URBANOVA Brand (the business)**                           | **URBANOVA Movement (the community and fund)**          |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|
| What it is             | Streetwear label and online store                           | Youth community, showcase, mentoring and support fund   |
| Money in               | Product sales, collaborations                               | Donations, sponsorships, grants, a share of brand sales |
| Money out              | Production, delivery, salaries, profit to owners            | Youth programs, support fund, events, running costs     |
| Legal form (suggested) | Company limited by shares (or sole proprietorship to start) | Company limited by guarantee (not-for-profit)           |
| Link between them      | Commits a set share of sales to the Movement                | Promotes the brand as a founding partner                |

*Why separate? A not-for-profit can receive grants and donations that a profit-making company usually cannot, and its surplus must be reinvested in its purpose rather than shared out. Section 11 covers registration.*

4\. What the Movement Does

Five programs, designed to start small and grow. Names are working titles.

| **Program**       | **What it does**                                                                                                                      | **Who it serves**                                  |
|-------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| Nova Spotlight    | Features young founders and creatives on the URBANOVA website and social channels: profile, story, photos, link to their shop or page | Any member with a real project, product or service |
| Nova Market       | A section of the site where member businesses can list products, with URBANOVA handling payment and a small commission                | Member founders with something to sell             |
| Nova Mentors      | Matches members with experienced business owners and partner staff for guidance sessions                                              | Members starting or growing a venture              |
| Nova Starter Fund | Small grants or in-kind help (equipment, materials, training) for members, awarded through a transparent process                      | Members with a plan and a need                     |
| Nova Nights       | Events: pop-up markets, pitch evenings, workshops, drops, meet-ups with partners                                                      | Members, partners, the public                      |

4.1 How Someone Joins

- Free membership sign-up on the website (name, age band, location, what they do or want to do)

- Members accept a short community code (Section 9.3)

- Members can then apply for Spotlight, Mentors, Market listing or the Starter Fund

- Applicants under 18 need a parent or guardian's consent for anything that publishes their image or details (Section 9.2)

5\. Youth Support & Showcase Policy

5.1 Encouraging Young People to Be Themselves

- Spotlight stories let members describe their own journey in their own words; we edit for length and clarity, not to change who they are

- No dress code, pitch style or “brand look” is imposed on members. URBANOVA gear is welcome, never required

- Peer features: members spotlight other members, so the community supports each other

- Plain, practical guidance at every step: how to price, how to photograph products, how to talk to customers

5.2 Broadcasting Members' Businesses

| **Channel**            | **What members get**                                                            |
|------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Website Spotlight page | Profile page with story, photos and a link to buy or contact                    |
| URBANOVA social media  | Feature posts, reels and stories on the movement's accounts, tagging the member |
| Nova Market listing    | Products sold through the URBANOVA site (once built)                            |
| Events                 | A table or stage slot at Nova Nights                                            |
| Newsletter             | Featured in the community newsletter to subscribers                             |

5.3 Fair Selection

- Publish simple criteria for Spotlight and Starter Fund (for example: real project, clear plan, genuine need, willingness to share their story)

- Decisions made by at least three people, not one, with a short written reason recorded

- No payment to be featured. Spotlight is earned by the work, not bought

- Give feedback to people who are not selected, and invite them to reapply

5.4 Ownership of Members' Work

- Members keep full ownership of their brand, designs and content

- Members give URBANOVA permission to share the agreed Spotlight content, and can ask for it to be removed at any time

6\. Partnership Model

Partners are businesses and organizations that want to back young people and reach the audience URBANOVA is building. The aim is a fair exchange: partners get visibility and goodwill; the movement gets money, goods, skills and credibility.

6.1 Partner Types

| **Type**               | **Examples**                                             | **What they can give**                                               |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Brand partners         | Local fashion, print, photography, music, food brands    | Collaborations, co-branded drops, shared events, product for members |
| Corporate sponsors     | Banks, telecoms, retailers, beverage companies           | Funding, event sponsorship, staff mentors                            |
| In-kind partners       | Printers, studios, courier firms, co-working spaces      | Services and materials instead of cash                               |
| Institutional partners | Schools, universities, youth agencies, NGOs, foundations | Referrals, venues, grants, training                                  |
| Community supporters   | Individuals and small businesses                         | Monthly gifts, one-off donations, volunteering                       |

6.2 Suggested Partnership Tiers

Established youth-enterprise charities group partners by giving level and recognize them accordingly (Section 10). A simple version for URBANOVA:

| **Tier**          | **Contribution (GHS, to be set)**             | **Recognition**                                                                |
|-------------------|-----------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Founding Partner  | To be decided (highest level, limited places) | Logo on website home page and event stage, named programs, joint announcements |
| Nova Partner      | To be decided                                 | Logo on Partners page, social media features, event presence                   |
| Community Partner | To be decided / in-kind                       | Name on Partners page, thank-you post                                          |
| Supporter         | Any amount, one-off or monthly                | Name on supporters list (optional)                                             |

6.3 How Partnering Works

- Partner interest form and a short partner pack (who we are, what we do, what you get, how money is used)

- Intro call, then a one-page partnership agreement covering what each side provides, duration, logo use and how either side can step back

- Quarterly update to partners: members supported, events held, money received and spent

6.4 Partner Standards

- Partners must be a registered, legitimate business or organization

- No partnership with businesses whose products or conduct harm young people (for example tobacco, gambling, or exploitative lending)

- Partners do not control which members are chosen for Spotlight or the Starter Fund

- Partner logos and claims are only used with written approval from both sides

7\. How the Movement Makes Money

Relying on one source is risky. YBI's review of youth-enterprise organizations found that groups with only grant income stalled when a funder left, and recommended building earned income alongside donations (Section 10). The plan below mixes several sources.

| **Source**             | **How it works**                                                                             | **Stage**          |
|------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------|
| Brand sales share      | URBANOVA Brand commits a fixed share of each sale (percentage to be decided) to the Movement | Launch             |
| Partner sponsorships   | Annual or per-event sponsorship from corporate and brand partners                            | Early              |
| Event income           | Tickets, stall fees and sponsorship at Nova Nights                                           | Early              |
| Nova Market commission | Small commission on member sales through the marketplace                                     | Growth             |
| Monthly supporters     | Recurring small gifts from the community                                                     | Early              |
| Crowdfunding drives    | Time-limited campaigns for a specific goal (for example, a starter-kit round)                | Early              |
| Grants                 | Applications to foundations and youth-enterprise programs                                    | After registration |
| In-kind support        | Free or discounted services, venues and materials                                            | Launch             |
| Paid services (later)  | Workshops, brand collaborations, content services for businesses                             | Growth             |

7.1 Suggested Split of Money Received (starting point)

*Placeholder figures for the founders to adjust once real income is known.*

| **Use**                                            | **Share** |
|----------------------------------------------------|-----------|
| Starter Fund and direct help for members           | 50%       |
| Programs (events, mentoring, Spotlight production) | 25%       |
| Running costs (tools, admin, accounting)           | 15%       |
| Reserve for slow months                            | 10%       |

7.2 Money Principles

- Donations and grants are used only for the purpose they were given for

- Movement money goes into a separate bank account, never the brand's or a founder's personal account

- Keep running costs low and publish the figure

- No single funder should supply most of the income for long. Aim to build several sources

8\. Raising Funds to Help People in Need

These are fundraising methods commonly used by youth and social enterprises (Section 10), matched to what URBANOVA can do.

8.1 Fundraising Playbook

- **Give-back product lines.** A designated tee or hoodie where a fixed amount per item goes to the fund, with the amount stated on the product page.

- **Monthly giving.** Invite supporters to give a small recurring amount via mobile money. Recurring gifts make planning easier. Send them regular updates so they see the effect.

- **Crowdfunding campaigns.** One clear goal, a realistic target, a story with visuals, and rewards at different levels (for example a thank-you shout-out, a sticker, a tee).

- **Fundraising events.** Pop-up markets, music nights, pitch evenings or sports days tied to the mission, with part of the income to the fund.

- **Corporate sponsorship.** Research companies that run community or youth programs, then offer clear benefits: brand exposure, recognition, staff volunteering.

- **Partner matching.** Ask a partner to match what the community raises, which encourages more people to give.

- **Grants.** Apply to foundations and programs that fund youth enterprise, once registered and with accounts to show.

- **Social media storytelling.** Share real stories of members helped, with their permission, and post progress toward goals.

8.2 Helping People Who Need It

Define clearly who qualifies, so help is fair and the fund is not overwhelmed.

- Starter help: tools, materials or a small grant to launch a member's idea

- Emergency help: a small, capped amount for a member facing a sudden setback that threatens their business or schooling (to be defined carefully)

- Training: sponsored places on skills courses

- Priority goes to members with least access to money or networks

*Start with a small, well-run fund and publish what was given. A fund that is open about results earns more trust than a large one that is vague.*

9\. Governance & Policies

9.1 Structure

- Founding team with defined roles: lead, finance/treasurer, programs, partnerships, communications

- Movement entity needs at least two directors and a company secretary under Ghana's rules (Section 11)

- A small advisory circle of respected local business owners and youth leaders (informal at first)

- Decisions about money above an agreed amount need two signatures

9.2 Child and Youth Safeguarding

- Many members will be under 18, so this policy is essential before launch

- Parent or guardian consent before publishing any under-18's image, name, school or location

- Two adults present or reachable in any mentoring contact with minors; no private one-to-one contact with minors on personal accounts

- Mentors and volunteers are screened and sign the code of conduct

- A named person is responsible for safeguarding, with a clear way to report concerns

9.3 Community Code of Conduct

- Respect others, no bullying, no hate speech, no harassment

- No fake products, scams or misleading claims by members

- Be honest about what you sell and what it costs

- Breaking the code can lead to removal from Spotlight or the community

9.4 Transparency & Accountability

- Keep proper books from day one; use a credible auditor (Ghana's registration process requires an auditor to be named)

- Publish a simple annual report: money in, money out, members supported, stories

- Show the give-back amount on products and report the total raised

9.5 Conflict of Interest

- Founders and relatives cannot receive Starter Fund money or decide on their own applications

- Partners cannot influence selections in exchange for money

- Declare any personal interest in a decision

9.6 Data & Privacy

- Collect only what is needed from members and supporters

- Keep data secure and do not sell or share it with partners without consent

- Follow Ghana's data protection requirements (to be confirmed with a legal adviser)

9.7 Honest Communication

- Never promise members income or guaranteed results

- Never use a member's story to raise money without their consent

- Describe what the fund can and cannot do

10\. What Other Movements Do (Research)

Findings from published material on established youth-enterprise organizations. Sources are listed in Section 14.

10.1 Futurpreneur (Canada): Tiered Corporate and Government Partners

- Funded by a mix of government bodies, major corporations and family foundations

- Partners are grouped by giving level (for example \$1,000,000+, \$500,000+, \$100,000+ and up to \$100,000) with logos shown under each tier

- Partners provide financial support or other resources; interested organizations contact the charity directly

- **What URBANOVA can borrow:** tiered recognition, a clear partner page and a simple route to get in touch.

10.2 Youth Business International: Lessons on Sustainability

- Support models used across the network include training, mentoring for several months, seed grants (about US\$500 in one example), savings groups and mobile banking, and partner networks

- One organization moved toward earned income from services, content and consultancy, which covered its core fixed costs

- Programs stalled when a funder's support ended; reliance on donors from a single sector was flagged as a risk

- Embedding programs in partners' existing budgets helped them continue after projects ended

- **What URBANOVA can borrow:** mix grants with earned income, keep mentoring structured, and avoid depending on one funder.

10.3 Ghana: Young Africa Works (GEA and Mastercard Foundation)

- The Ghana Enterprises Agency runs a youth entrepreneurship and employment program with the Mastercard Foundation, aiming to create jobs and income for 39,000 young people, with a focus on young women

- **What URBANOVA can borrow:** Ghana already has public and foundation programs for youth enterprise. The movement can refer members to these, and apply to partner with them.

10.4 Fundraising Methods for Young Social Entrepreneurs

- Common methods: social media storytelling, creative events tied to the mission, strategic partnerships, crowdfunding with rewards at different levels, monthly giving programs, and corporate sponsorship tied to a company's community programs

- **What URBANOVA can borrow:** all of these fit a youth streetwear brand; Section 8 sets out how.

*Note: these are summaries of public materials. Before approaching any funder, read their current eligibility rules directly, as programs change.*

11\. Registering in Ghana

This section summarizes a published 2023 legal guide to registering a not-for-profit in Ghana. Fees and forms change, so confirm everything with the Registrar General's Department (RGD) and a lawyer or accountant before filing.

11.1 Suggested Legal Forms

- Brand arm: a registered business (sole proprietorship or company limited by shares), depending on advice about liability and tax

- Movement arm: a company limited by guarantee, the structure generally used for social, charitable or community causes, where surplus is reinvested rather than shared out

11.2 What the RGD Asks For (Not-for-Profit)

- A proposed name, with at least two alternatives for the name search

- Registered office, principal place of business and digital address

- Tax Identification Numbers for the subscribers, directors and secretary (Ghana Card for Ghanaians)

- At least two initial directors and a secretary, with at least one director or the secretary resident in Ghana

- Name, address and consent letter of an auditor

- Application forms (company details, director and secretary consents, statutory declaration, beneficial ownership forms) and the statutory fee. The guide quoted GH₵270 in 2023; check the current fee

11.3 Steps

- 1\. Choose and search the name (check it is not similar to an existing one)

- 2\. Get TINs for everyone involved

- 3\. Complete and submit the forms with supporting documents

- 4\. Receive the certificate, then open a separate Movement bank account

- 5\. Ask an accountant about tax treatment, as the guide notes most not-for-profits are generally outside the tax net, but does not explain how to obtain exemption

*Not legal advice. The source also contained a statute-reference inconsistency, so rely on a qualified professional.*

- Donation option using the same payment gateway, kept clearly separate from product orders in the database
